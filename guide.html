Music Theory in Crayon

Everything you need to make music with Bangerator, explained like you’re five (but a very cool five). Press the green ▶ buttons to hear the examples.

The big idea Most music apps make you think in note names like C, F♯, B♭. Bangerator uses numbers instead: 1 is “home” and the other numbers are steps away from home. Numbers work the same in every key, so a tune you learn once works everywhere.

1What is a note?

Sound is air wiggling. When something wiggles fast, we hear a high sound. When it wiggles slow, we hear a low sound. How high or low a sound is gets called its pitch.

A note is just a sound with a steady pitch. A whistle, a sung “laaa”, a guitar string: those are notes. A hand clap isn’t really a note because it has no steady pitch. That’s why drums get their own grid in Bangerator.

2The 12-step staircase

Western music uses 12 different notes, and then they repeat. Think of a staircase with 12 steps. When you reach the top, you’re on the same “color” of step again, just one floor higher.

On a piano, the 12 steps are the white and black keys together. Going from one key to the very next one (white or black) is the smallest step. We call it a half step.

Crayon version Octave = climbing all 12 steps. The note at the top sounds like the bottom one, just “smaller”. A child and an adult singing the same song together are an octave apart.

3Half steps and whole steps

  • Half step (H) = move 1 key. Tiny and squeezy.
  • Whole step (W) = move 2 keys (two half steps). A normal-sized step.

Almost all of music is built out of these two sizes.

4Scales are recipes

You don’t use all 12 notes at once, because that sounds messy. A scale is a recipe that picks 7 of the 12 steps (sometimes 5 or 6). The recipe is a list of step sizes.

The Major scale recipe is:

W  W  H  W  W  W  H

Start on any key, follow the recipe, and you get a major scale. It always sounds “major”, just higher or lower.

5Why numbers? (Scale degrees)

Each note in a scale gets a number: the first note is 1, the next is 2, all the way to 7. After 7 comes 1 again, one octave up. These numbers are called scale degrees.

Crayon version 1 is your house. The other numbers are places in your neighborhood. A song is a walk around the neighborhood. Most songs end back at the house, because that feels finished.

The key tells you where the house is (which of the 12 notes is 1). The scale tells you what the neighborhood looks like.

A melody written in numbers, like 1 1 5 5 6 6 5, sounds like the same tune in every key. That’s “Twinkle Twinkle”, by the way:

In Bangerator every row on the grid is a number, not a note name. Change the key and your whole song moves with it, nothing breaks.

6Happy and sad: major and minor

The Natural Minor recipe is W H W W H W W. Compared to major, three notes are one half step lower: ♭3, ♭6 and ♭7. The little ♭ (“flat”) means “one half step lower than in major”. ♯ (“sharp”) means one half step higher.

Crayon version The 3 is the mood switch. A big 3 (major 3rd) sounds like a smile. A small ♭3 (minor 3rd) sounds like a frown. Swap just that one note and the mood flips.

In Bangerator, the small grey hint next to each row number (like ♭3) tells you how that note compares to the major scale.

7Modes: the mood ladder

Here’s a magic trick. Take the white keys of a piano, but decide that a different key is “home”. You get a new scale with a new mood. These are the modes. Bangerator has them all, and the sidebar has ☀ Brighter and ☾ Darker buttons to climb the ladder:

Every mode changes just one note from its neighbor on the ladder. That’s why swapping scales on the same loop in Bangerator works so well: most of your notes stay put, and one or two shift to change the color.

Try this in Bangerator Make a loop in Minor. Press ☀ to go to Dorian: the 6 gets brighter and it suddenly sounds funky. Press ☾ to go to Phrygian: the 2 gets darker and it sounds like a villain entering.

8Pentatonic: the “can’t go wrong” scales

Penta means five. A pentatonic scale leaves out the two “spiciest” notes, the ones that can clash. What’s left is 5 notes that all sound nice together, in any order.

  • Major pentatonic: 1 2 3 5 6. Happy, folky, pop.
  • Minor pentatonic: 1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7. Rock solos, hip-hop hooks, blues.
  • Blues: minor pentatonic plus the “blue note” ♭5 (in Bangerator it shows up as ♯4). Gritty.
Beginner tip If a melody isn’t working, switch to a pentatonic scale. Bangerator moves each note to the nearest pentatonic note. Often the wrong-sounding note just disappears.

9Chords: stacking every other number

A chord is several notes played at the same time. The easy recipe: pick a number, skip one, take one, skip one, take one.

Start on 1 → take 1, 3, 5. That’s the 1 chord. Start on 2 → 2, 4, 6. That’s the 2 chord. And so on. A three-note chord like this is called a triad.

The cool part You don’t choose which chord is major or minor. The scale decides. In the major scale, stacking gives: 1 major, 2 minor, 3 minor, 4 major, 5 major, 6 minor, 7 diminished. Change the scale and the chord flavors change by themselves. That’s why swapping scales changes the whole vibe.

10Roman numerals: chord numbers

Musicians write chord numbers with Roman numerals so they don’t mix them up with melody numbers:

1234567
IIIIIIIVVVIVII
  • BIG letters (IV) = major chord (happy).
  • small letters (vi) = minor chord (sad).
  • ° (vii°) = diminished (spooky).
  • ♭ in front (♭VII) = the chord’s root is a half step lower than in the major scale.

So in major the chords are: I ii iii IV V vi vii°. In natural minor: i ii° ♭III iv v ♭VI ♭VII.

When somebody says “it’s a I–V–vi–IV song”, you now know exactly what to play in any key. Bangerator shows these numerals on every chord.

11Home, Away, Tension

Chords have jobs. Bangerator colors them by job:

12Chord progressions and loops

A chord progression is a list of chords in order. In electronic music and pop, it’s usually a loop of 4 chords, one per bar, over and over.

Bangerator’s Famous progressions list adds any of these with one click. Because they’re stored as numbers, you can then swap the scale and hear the same progression in a new mood.

Neat trick I–V–vi–IV and vi–IV–I–V are the same four chords, just starting at a different point in the loop. Starting on the sad one (vi) makes the whole thing feel sad. Where you start the loop matters!

13The circle of fifths

The circle of fifths is a map of the neighborhood of keys. All 12 keys sit on a clock. Going one step clockwise moves you up 5 scale steps (a “fifth”, 7 half steps).

Crayon version Keys that are next to each other on the circle are best friends: they share 6 of their 7 notes. Keys on opposite sides are strangers and share very few notes.

How to read the circle in Bangerator

  • Outer ring: major chords/keys. Middle ring: minor. Inner ring: diminished.
  • The chords in your current key light up, colored by their job (Home, Away, Tension), with their Roman numeral. The white outline is home (I).
  • In major, the lit chords sit together in a neat wedge: IV is on the left of I, V on the right, and the minor chords right below them.
  • The little dots on the rim show your scale’s notes. For major they make one unbroken arc of 7.
  • Click any key to move your song there. Same numbers, new key!

Relative major and minor

Each major key has a minor twin directly below it on the circle (C major ↔ A minor). They use the exact same notes, just with a different home. That’s why A minor is 6 of C major.

Key signatures (the ♯ and ♭ count)

Going clockwise adds a ♯ to the key, going counter-clockwise adds a ♭. C major has none, G has 1♯, D has 2♯… F has 1♭, B♭ has 2♭… The center of the circle shows the count for your key.

Why fifths?

The 5 is the strongest pull back to 1. Many songs move around the circle (counter-clockwise, falling by fifths): vi → ii → V → I. Each chord is the “V” of the next one, like dominoes.

14Rhythm: beats, bars and steps

  • Beat: the pulse you tap your foot to.
  • BPM (beats per minute): how fast the beat goes. 70 = chill, 90–100 = hip-hop, 120–128 = house, 140 = dubstep/trap (feels like 70), 170+ = drum & bass.
  • Bar (or measure): a group of 4 beats. Count “1 2 3 4, 1 2 3 4”.
  • Step: Bangerator splits each beat into 4 tiny steps (16th notes). So one bar = 16 steps. The grid lines get thicker every beat and every bar.
  • Loop length: how many bars before the song repeats. 4 or 8 bars is the classic.

Swing

Normally all 16 steps are exactly the same length. Swing makes every second step a little late, like “long-short, long-short”. It turns stiff robot beats into bouncy human grooves. Try 10–30% swing on hip-hop or house.

Crayon rules of drums Kick = the heartbeat (usually on beat 1). Snare/clap = the “answer”, on beats 2 and 4 (or just beat 3 in half-time trap). Hi-hats = the ticking clock that keeps everything moving. Start with these three, then add sprinkles (toms, cowbell, samples).

Accents: shift-click (or right-click) a step to make it an extra-loud hit. Mixing loud and soft hi-hats makes a beat feel alive.

16Basslines

The bass is the glue between the drums and the chords. The simplest bassline that always works: play the chord’s root number (the chord’s own 1), low, in the rhythm of the kick drum.

  • Chord is vi? Play degree 6 in the bass. Chord is IV? Play 4.
  • For extra movement, jump to the chord’s 5th, or to the same note an octave up.
  • For 808 bass: long notes, one per kick, sliding between roots.

In Bangerator, the rows tinted in a chord’s color are the chord’s notes. The brightest tinted row is the root, and that’s the safest bass note.

17Writing melodies

  1. Land on chord notes on strong beats. Tinted rows = notes of the current chord. Put those on beats 1 and 3 and the melody fits the chords.
  2. Walk more than you jump. Moving to the next number (3 → 4) sounds smooth. Big leaps (1 → 6) are exciting, so use them now and then, and step back afterwards.
  3. Repeat, then change a little. Say it twice, then twist the ending. Listeners love recognizing things.
  4. Leave gaps. Silence is part of the melody. Let it breathe.
  5. End on 1 (or 3 or 5) to sound finished. End on 2 or 7 to sound like a question.

Stuck? Write a melody first, then press ✨ Chords for my melody and Bangerator picks chords that contain your notes.

Accidentals (♯ and ♭ notes)

Sometimes you want a note that isn’t in the scale, for a moment of color. Shift+click a note in Bangerator to raise it (♯) or lower it (♭). Use these sparingly, like hot sauce.

18Sound design: waves and envelopes

The four basic waves

Every synth sound starts as a simple wave. Each shape has its own personality:

WaveSounds likeGood for
SinePure, round, a whistle or a humSub bass, 808s, soft tones
TriangleSoft, flute-y, a sine with a little edgeGentle leads, plucks
SquareHollow, buzzy, retro video gameChiptune, leads, woody bass
SawBright, buzzy, fullBig leads, pads, basses (the EDM classic)

Smooth waves (sine) have no extra “fizz”. Sharp-cornered waves (square, saw) are full of extra high sounds called harmonics, and that’s the fizz you hear.

The envelope: ADSR

An envelope is the life story of one note: how it’s born, lives and dies.

LetterNameCrayon meaning
AAttackHow fast it gets loud. Short = punchy (drum, pluck). Long = slow swell (pad, strings).
DDecayHow fast it drops after the first hit.
SSustainHow loud it stays while you hold the note. 0% = it fades away even if you hold it (piano, pluck).
RReleaseHow long it rings after you let go. Short = tight, long = echo-y tail.

The filter

A low-pass filter is like putting your hand over the speaker: it removes the bright fizz. Turning the cutoff down makes the sound darker. Resonance adds a whistle right at the cutoff point. Filter pluck opens the filter at the start of each note and closes it again. That “wow” is behind most synth basses.

Pitch drop (808 style)

If each note starts higher and quickly slides down, a sine wave turns into a booming 808 kick-bass. Bangerator’s “808 Sub” instrument works this way.

19What is MIDI?

MIDI is sheet music for computers. It doesn’t contain any sound. It’s a list of instructions like “press note 60, this hard, now… let go now”. Any instrument can play it.

  • Export MIDI saves your song as a .mid file. Drag it into FL Studio, Ableton, Logic or GarageBand and pick any instruments you like.
  • MIDI output sends notes live to a DAW or hardware synth while Bangerator plays (Chrome/Edge).

Note numbers in MIDI: 60 is middle C, and +1 is one half step up, so +12 is one octave up.
